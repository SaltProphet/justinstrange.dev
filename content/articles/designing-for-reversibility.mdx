## The useful middle

Early decisions are often treated as either permanent commitments or disposable guesses. There is a more useful middle: decisions with enough shape to move the work forward and enough seams to be revisited.

Reversibility is not indecision. It is the deliberate placement of boundaries where learning is likely to change the answer.

## Three questions

When making a decision, ask what would make it wrong, what evidence would change the choice, and how expensive it would be to change course. The answers are more valuable than a confident label.

> A good early architecture does not predict the future. It keeps the cost of being surprised acceptable.

## A small practice

Record the decision, its constraints, its evidence, and its expiry condition. Revisit it when the system crosses that condition rather than when anxiety asks for another diagram.
